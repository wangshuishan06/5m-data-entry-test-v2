# Question 6 — AI Literacy: Prompt Engineering

**Format:** Short Answer

---

Read both prompts below carefully, then answer the questions that follow.

**Prompt A**

> I am a marketing manager at a retail company and we have just finished a three-month campaign. My team has collected customer feedback through an online survey and we now have about 500 responses stored in a spreadsheet. Each response includes the customer's age group, the product they purchased, their satisfaction rating from 1 to 5, and a short written comment. I need to present the findings to our CEO next Friday in a way that is easy to understand. Can you analyse this data for me, highlight which age groups and products have the lowest satisfaction scores, identify the most common complaints from the written comments, and summarise everything in a short paragraph I can use as an executive summary?

**Prompt B**

> **Role:** You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.  
> **Task:** Analyse customer survey data from a 3-month campaign.  
> **Data:** 500 responses containing age group, product purchased, satisfaction rating (1-5), and written comments.  
> **Steps:**
> 1. Identify age groups and products with the lowest satisfaction scores.
> 2. Extract the most common themes from the written comments.
> 3. Summarise findings in an executive summary paragraph.
>
> **Audience:** CEO presentation on Friday.  
> **Constraints:** Keep the summary concise and free of technical jargon.

---

## Task 1 — Analysis

**(a)** Which prompt do you think will get a better response from an AI? State your choice (A or B).

**Answer:**  
Prompt B
---

**(b)** Give **two reasons** to support your choice.

**Reason 1:**  
>Structured Format and Clear Task Decomposition: Prompt B uses modular sections and numbered steps. This layout follow the required workflow sequentially, and ensure no sub-task overlooked.
**Reason 2:**  
>Explicit Constraints and Audience Framing: Prompt B explicitly defines target audience (CEO) and actionable negative constraints. This directly quides the AI to calibrate its tone, vocabulary ,and conciseness specifically for executively level communication.
---

**(c)** What is **one strength** of the prompt you did NOT choose?

**Answer:**  
```
Clearer Intent on Negative Feedback: Prompt A specifically directs the model to identify "the most common complaints", whereas Prompt B more neutrally asks for "common themes". By focusing on complaints, Prompt A better aligns with the goal of diagnosing the lowest satisfaction scores.
```

## Task 2 — Rewrite

Rewrite either prompt by borrowing **one element** from the other to make it stronger. Then explain what you borrowed and why.

**Your rewritten prompt:**

> _Role: You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.
Task: Analyse customer survey data from a 3-month campaign.
Data: 500 responses containing age group, product purchased, satisfaction rating (1-5), and written comments.
Steps:
1.Identify age groups and products with the lowest satisfaction scores.
2.Extract the most common complaints and pain points from the written comments.
3.Summarise findings in an executive summary paragraph suitable for slide presentation.
Audience: CEO presentation on Friday.
Constraints: Keep the summary concise, easy to understand, and free of technical jargon.

**What you borrowed:**  
_(I borrowed the explicit focus on "complaints" (negative feedback) from Prompt A to replace the generic term "themes" in Step 2 of Prompt B.)_

**why:**  
_In Prompt B, asking for "themes" is too broad—it could lead the AI to highlight neutral or positive praise. Since Step 1 specifically focuses on the lowest satisfaction scores, borrowing Prompt A’s focus on customer complaints ensures that the qualitative comment analysis directly explains the root causes behind those low ratings, making the final executive summary much more actionable for the CEO._
